My gurus

Learning from the masters

In Indian classical music, knowledge passes from guru to shishya, student, through years of close listening, imitation and guidance. I am fortunate to learn from two accomplished flautists from Bengaluru, one in each tradition, and from my gurus in rhythm.

PH

HINDUSTANI FLUTE (BANSURI)

Pandit Shri Prakash Hegde

Hindustani bansuri performer and teacher · Bengaluru

Panditji hails from Kallaremane, a village in Siddapur taluk of Uttara Kannada, Karnataka. He received his first lessons on the flute from Vidwan Prabhakar Bhat in Sirsi, refined his playing under the renowned flautist the late Pandit Venkatesh Godkhindi of Dharwad, and later received guidance from the internationally acclaimed flautist Pandit Raghunath Seth of Mumbai.

Highlights

  • Graded 'A' by Akashvani (All India Radio) as a Hindustani bansuri artist
  • Joint first prize at a national-level instrumental music competition
  • Performs solo, as an accompanist for dance and devotional music, in jugalbandi, and in fusion with Indian and international artists
  • Founder of the Swara Ganga Flute Academy, teaching students from around the world
What I learnWith Panditji I explore the unhurried unfolding of a raga, breath and tone on the bansuri, and the Hindustani approach to improvisation.
RK

CARNATIC FLUTE (VENU)

Vidwan Ravichandra Kulur

Internationally acclaimed flautist · Bengaluru

Vidwan Kulur began his training under his father, Sri K. Raghavendra Rao, himself a flautist, and also learned mridangam and kanjira as a child. He gave his first solo recital at the age of twelve. Pandit Ravi Shankar hailed him as a flute maestro and invited him to perform in the 'Festival of India III' concerts of 2005-2006.

Highlights

  • More than 1,200 concerts in 34 countries, including Carnegie Hall, the Kennedy Center, Royal Festival Hall and Sydney Opera House
  • Festivals including WOMAD, Roskilde, Helsinki Festival and Bangalore Habba
  • Collaborations with Pandit Ravi Shankar, Anoushka Shankar and A. R. Rahman
  • Music that blends Carnatic tradition with world music and jazz fusion
What I learnWith Vidwan Kulur I study the gamakas and compositions of Carnatic music. His path, from percussion to flute and from Carnatic roots to the world stage, is one I find especially inspiring.
